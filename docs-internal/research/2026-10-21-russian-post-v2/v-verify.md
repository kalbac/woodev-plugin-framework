Exactly **five further authenticated calls** precede that response: the 180-band checkpoint poll, the main-script final poll, and tail polls 0–2; the intervening point/price/market queries were anonymous. The before/after totals fit the one-unit model for all fourteen non-limit authenticated probes, including the passport download and failed HTTP 400. ## contradictions

### Must fix before using the research as an implementation specification

1. **What:** B §2 calls 53030 “verified live” on the basis of HTTP 200 and presents 53030/53070 as current tariff codes; E/G correctly report their retirement. **Problem:** my fresh 53030/53070 responses also have HTTP 200 but carry business error 1204, so a successful HTTP status is being confused with a successful price. **Fix:** mark the old codes historical/unavailable for tariffing and require a price plus no business errors. **If skipped:** the new plugin can offer a retired product or turn an API error into a zero/missing rate.

2. **What:** E §3.2/§7 suggests postamat pickup uses only 54020, whereas G §4.2 identifies ONLINE_PARCEL COMBINED products 23080/23090. **Problem:** my fresh 23080 postamat calculation succeeds; 23080 is cheaper than 54020 in this declared-value comparison. **Fix:** retain a separate postamat-only ONLINE_PARCEL COMBINED branch and the 54020 partner/postamat branch. **If skipped:** valid postamat shipping options disappear or the wrong product is selected.

3. **What:** B §2 and E §7 prescribe `delivery-data.delivery-point-index` for pickup order creation, while G §8.1 reports a successful ONLINE_PARCEL COMBINED postamat order only with `ecom-data.delivery-point-index` plus `dimension-type`. **Problem:** the supposedly general recipe conflicts with the later successful write experiment. **Fix:** annotate the product-specific exception and keep other pickup order payloads unverified; do not flatten them into one recipe. **If skipped:** postamat order exports can fail even though their tariffs calculate. I did not repeat writes, per this verifier's read-only scope.

4. **What:** E §4 first says widget→Otpravka mirroring is UNVERIFIED, then states widget-only merchants have **no Otpravka batch history**. **Problem:** the second statement assumes the unresolved answer is “no.” **Fix:** make the history-loss/migration statement conditional until an actual mirrored order is inspected. **If skipped:** migration can be designed around a false absence of existing orders.

5. **What:** C headline says the market is stale/only the aggregator is maintained, while C's Saphali row explicitly has **no verified update date**; the stronger “none exists” wording also exceeds a wp.org sample. **Problem:** unknown commercial maintenance is treated as demonstrated abandonment. **Fix:** say “no maintained dedicated alternative was verified among the checked products” and preserve Saphali/commercial-market uncertainty. **If skipped:** product planning overstates the evidence of an empty market.

6. **What:** A headline #3 says CMS calls bypass the cache, implying a clean exception to caching. **Problem:** `bypass_cache()` sets `force_refresh=true` and `should_cache=false`, but bundled `abstract-cacheable-api-base.php:44–45,79–85` never tests `should_cache()` when saving. My harness makes two bypassed CMS-style calls: **two network calls, one persisted cache entry with TTL 86400**. **Fix:** describe it as bypassing **cache reads**, with writes still occurring; the PUT/DELETE finding remains valid. **If skipped:** a future port can carry forward the incorrect assumption that the flag prevents storage.

7. **What:** F §3.2 explains the +20 points versus E as geographic coverage (19 negative-longitude Chukotka, one below 41°N), but F §3.3 speculates that the same +20 is “normal churn” or timing. **Problem:** a geometric explanation is already demonstrated; describing it as churn obscures the coverage bug. **Fix:** keep the measured geographic explanation and remove the competing speculation. **If skipped:** future crawls can again silently omit remote points.

8. **What:** F §0/§2 reports byte-identical responses for settings-ID variants. **Problem:** my two equivalent central-Moscow requests have identical 123-record maps and metadata but different array order. **Fix:** scope byte equality to F's observed requests and use ID-normalised equality as the invariant. **If skipped:** a harmless response reordering can be mistaken for changed coverage/account filtering.

9. **What:** B's all-surface inventory says “Webhooks / callbacks: None found,” while A/E document and cite the plugin's CMS barcode/status callbacks. **Problem:** the claim is only defensible for the checked **Otpravka/tracking** surface, not the CMS system included elsewhere in B. **Fix:** explicitly scope “none found” to Otpravka/tracking and list the CMS callbacks separately. **If skipped:** dropping CMS functionality can be misrepresented as losing no push updates.

**Verdict:** the passport source, anonymous widget coverage, sampled price parity, mixed-bootstrap guards and v1 red flags are independently supported. Fix contradictions 1–4 before turning the reports into a plugin implementation specification; do not treat this test account as evidence of real-contract discounts or all pickup order capabilities.
