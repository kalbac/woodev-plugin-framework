## #526 — select2's UI strings now come from `wc_country_select_params`

The reported symptom was «No results found» in English on a Russian checkout, in the
`ajax-select2` settlement mode. The cause: `config.language` was only ever wired on the **non-ajax**
branch of `selectConfigFor()`, and only when `seed.onAbandon` happened to be a function.

**The reported string was not the worst of it.** `minimumInputLengthFor()` floors the settlement
field at 2, so `inputTooShort` — «Please enter 2 or more characters» — is what the customer stares
at *before typing anything*, on every visit. More constant than the defect that got reported, and
nobody had noticed it.

The operator's ruling on the card set the approach: do not invent translations, take WooCommerce's
own. `select2LanguageFor()` sources all seven messages from `wc_country_select_params`, copied
key-for-key from WooCommerce's `assets/js/frontend/country-select.js` **read in the rig container**,
not recalled. `Checkout_Handler` declares `wc-country-select` as a dependency, which is what makes
the global both present and printed first.

Three judgement calls, all deliberate:

1. **Wired for every strategy**, not just ajax — a `related-list` region field carrying no
   `onAbandon` hit the same untranslated defaults.
2. **`errorLoading` returns `i18n_searching`, not `i18n_ajax_error`.** Looks like a bug in the
   reference; is not. WooCommerce's documented workaround for select2/select2#4355. Pinned by a
   test so a future reader does not "fix" it.
3. **`noResults` prefers our own `emptyText`** over WooCommerce's generic `i18n_no_matches` —
   otherwise the two settlement modes would disagree about the same outcome.

## The critic earned its keep — on the one claim I flagged as weakest

The brief told Codex which claim I least trusted (C5: "returning `undefined` makes select2 fall
back to its own English"). It refuted exactly that one, and it was right.

`selectWoo.full.js:2236,4934-4940` — `customTranslation.extend( baseTranslation )` is
`$.extend( {}, EN, ours )`. **Ours wins for every key we define.** So a defined callback shadows
English permanently, and returning `undefined` renders a *blank* message box — strictly worse than
the English it was meant to avoid. The fix is to **omit** the key.

I re-measured the refutation against the selectWoo source before acting on it rather than taking
the critic's word, which is the standing rule; it held.

New gotcha: `a-select2-language-callback-that-returns-undefined-renders-blank`.

**A re-critic on the repair then refuted three more things — all of them comments, none of them
behaviour.** The blank-message corner was called unreachable (it needs two public filters abused at
once — rare, not impossible); "both plural branches are reachable" was claimed for all three plural
keys when only `inputTooShort` is live here; and the test comment blamed a dequeue route the critic
could not demonstrate, when the real one is `woocommerce_get_script_data`. Fixed as comment-only.

**That is now four times on this one file that an inference was recorded in a docblock as a fact.**
Each time it closed the question for the next reader. The countermeasure that actually worked here
was naming the weakest claim in the critic's brief and telling it to attack that specifically.

## #530 — the popular list finally reaches the customer

#488 shipped storage, clocks, lazy verification and merchant tools (D1–D8). The spec's own promise
— *"something useful before the customer types"* — never became a D-decision, so it was never built.
Nothing read the store on the checkout, which is why the operator could not exercise it at all.

**Design fork settled by measurement, before any code:** a config block, not a REST route.
`minimumInputLengthFor('settlement') === 2` means the empty state must exist at ATTACH time, `/suggest`
answers in 6–10 s so a round-trip there is unaffordable, and the list is capped at 30 rows.

Delegated to a Sonnet worker in its own worktree. **It corrected my brief's central assumption** —
`Location_Record::region()` carries only `{name, type}`, not a key; `ancestors()` is the real
mechanism, matching `is_within()`. Licensing the worker to contradict the brief keeps paying.

### Round 1 passed every gate and was broken twice over

Both defects appeared only for a **fresh customer in an isolated browser context** — the state the
feature exists for, and the one neither of us had tested.

1. **The field arrived pre-selected to the most popular city.** `selectedIndex 1`, chip reading
   «Санкт-Петербург» for someone who had never touched it, and that value posts. The class of bug
   #350 and #502 exist for. Cause, found in real Chrome rather than jsdom: the popular `<option>`s
   were appended with none carrying `.selected`, so the browser's own reset-selectedness algorithm
   took the first, and the blank option arrived too late to win.
2. **The popular list was never rendered.** Options in the DOM, dropdown saying «Please enter 2 or
   more characters». **Seeding real `<option>`s is not sufficient in ajax mode** — that was wrong in
   the card's own warning and wrong in my brief. `related-list` renders DOM options because it is a
   LOCAL select2; the ajax strategy uses its own data adapter and enforces the floor. Fixed by
   scoping `minimumInputLength` to 0 for a level carrying a popular list and answering the empty
   term locally, with no network call.

Round 2 fixed both, verified live: `selectedIndex 0`, chip «Выберите…», and six popular cities
listed with nothing typed.

Checking region filtering, I changed the region, waited **3000 ms**, read the dropdown, saw all six
entries, and filed **#534** calling region filtering structurally impossible — with a tidy
explanation from WooCommerce state codes and `ancestors` that convincingly accounted for it.

The round-trip on this rig is **6–10 s**. I had read the list before the change landed. Re-measured
with a control: Москва → Внуково/Москва/Бутово, Санкт-Петербург → СПб/Пушкин/Репино. It works.
#534 closed as `not planned`, PR description rewritten.

**s92's handoff carried this exact warning** («ждать по факту появления строки, а не по таймеру»).
Knowing it did not prevent it. What prevents it is method: poll for the expected state, and always
flip to a second value as a **control** — an unfiltered list and a not-yet-arrived list are the same
bytes, so one region proves nothing. New gotcha:
`a-rig-measurement-on-a-timer-invents-a-defect-that-is-not-there`.

The through-line of the whole session: **a confident, well-explained claim is the dangerous kind.**
It happened in a docblock (#526's C5), in three comments (the re-critic), and in a card of mine
(#534). Each time the explanation was elegant and the conclusion was wrong.

## #536 — the default locality nobody could see

The operator asked mid-session whether «город по умолчанию» worked. It did not, and it never had.
Measured on clean `main`: the server resolved «Москва» and rates were calculated from it, while the
customer got three empty fields and a locked address. **Not a regression** — the half-fix from
#502/#509 closed "an implicit default must not unlock the address" and left "the default is
invisible" without a card.

**I then made the session's worst mistake.** I found D11 in the spec, which said implicit records
"are not treated as the customer's answer", and produced it as an argument against his own fresh
decision. His correction: *«спеку то не я пишу а ты… ты в какой-то из сессий записал не так,
возможно додумал, неверно трактовал. А сейчас дошло до реализации и делаешь по спеке всё чётко»*.

He is right, and it generalizes: **a spec D-decision is the agent's TRANSCRIPT of him, never
authority over him.** Where the two disagree he wins and the spec gets corrected. Recorded in the
spec itself, above the amendment, so the next reader cannot repeat it.

Decision implemented: `fixed` is shown exactly as if the customer picked it; `geoip` stays
invisible. The distinction that was missing is that `implicit` covered two states —
*implicit-because-we-guessed* and *implicit-because-the-merchant-configured-it*.

Round 1 passed 2798 tests and did not work. The blocker: the checkout config sent only `{key}` for
the chain, so the client had **no text to write**. Round 2 killed my own hypothesis — the region
field is a plain `<input>` at `prefill()` time and the write succeeds; WooCommerce's
`country-select.js` later promotes it to a `<select>`, its value-based restore fails on the
UPPERCASE convention, and the resulting empty `change` makes `clearDescendants()` wipe the
already-correct settlement. Fixed by deferring the write to `country_to_state_changed`.
