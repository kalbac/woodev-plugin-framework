## #526 — select2's UI strings now come from `wc_country_select_params`

The reported symptom was «No results found» in English on a Russian checkout, in the
`ajax-select2` settlement mode. The cause: `config.language` was only ever wired on the **non-ajax**
branch of `selectConfigFor()`, and only when `seed.onAbandon` happened to be a function.

**The reported string was not the worst of it.** `minimumInputLengthFor()` floors the settlement
field at 2, so `inputTooShort` — «Please enter 2 or more characters» — is what the customer stares
at *before typing anything*, on every visit. More constant than the defect that got reported, and
nobody had noticed it.

The operator's ruling on the card set the approach: do not invent translations, take WooCommerce's
own. `select2LanguageFor()` sources all seven messages from `wc_country_select_params`, copied
key-for-key from WooCommerce's `assets/js/frontend/country-select.js` **read in the rig container**,
not recalled. `Checkout_Handler` declares `wc-country-select` as a dependency, which is what makes
the global both present and printed first.

Three judgement calls, all deliberate:

1. **Wired for every strategy**, not just ajax — a `related-list` region field carrying no
   `onAbandon` hit the same untranslated defaults.
2. **`errorLoading` returns `i18n_searching`, not `i18n_ajax_error`.** Looks like a bug in the
   reference; is not. WooCommerce's documented workaround for select2/select2#4355. Pinned by a
   test so a future reader does not "fix" it.
3. **`noResults` prefers our own `emptyText`** over WooCommerce's generic `i18n_no_matches` —
   otherwise the two settlement modes would disagree about the same outcome.

## The critic earned its keep — on the one claim I flagged as weakest

The brief told Codex which claim I least trusted (C5: "returning `undefined` makes select2 fall
back to its own English"). It refuted exactly that one, and it was right.

`selectWoo.full.js:2236,4934-4940` — `customTranslation.extend( baseTranslation )` is
`$.extend( {}, EN, ours )`. **Ours wins for every key we define.** So a defined callback shadows
English permanently, and returning `undefined` renders a *blank* message box — strictly worse than
the English it was meant to avoid. The fix is to **omit** the key.

I re-measured the refutation against the selectWoo source before acting on it rather than taking
the critic's word, which is the standing rule; it held.

New gotcha: `a-select2-language-callback-that-returns-undefined-renders-blank`.

**A re-critic on the repair then refuted three more things — all of them comments, none of them
behaviour.** The blank-message corner was called unreachable (it needs two public filters abused at
once — rare, not impossible); "both plural branches are reachable" was claimed for all three plural
keys when only `inputTooShort` is live here; and the test comment blamed a dequeue route the critic
could not demonstrate, when the real one is `woocommerce_get_script_data`. Fixed as comment-only.

**That is now four times on this one file that an inference was recorded in a docblock as a fact.**
Each time it closed the question for the next reader. The countermeasure that actually worked here
was naming the weakest claim in the critic's brief and telling it to attack that specifically.

## Verification that actually proved something

The canary test had a **control**, which is what made it evidence rather than a coincidence:
a canary written into `i18n_input_too_short_n` rendered as «CANARY-526 введите ещё 2» with `%qty%`
substituted, while the **un-canaried** `i18n_input_too_short_1` kept WooCommerce's real value.

The zero-result render was asserted on the **rendered text**, not a callback return value —
«Поиск не дал результатов. Попробуйте изменить запрос.» That distinction is a standing correction
from earlier sessions and it was needed here: the callback returned the right string while the
rendered dropdown still showed a tag row, because the option was on.
