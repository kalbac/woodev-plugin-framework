## Decisions

1. **The warning level is armed per-sniff, not per-file.** Silencing 19 sniffs with one argument to
   avoid 1397 line-length reports cost visibility into 16 other sniffs. The exclusions now carry
   reasons, as the ~80 error-level ones already did.
2. **A deliberately-silenced rule must still be measurable, and that needs its own ruleset file** —
   proven necessary, not chosen for tidiness. See the gotcha.
3. **A one-off legitimate violation gets an inline `phpcs:ignore` with a reason, never a global
   exclusion.** A global exclusion disarms the sniff for all future code; the point of the card was
   that a rule enforced by nothing is not a rule.
4. **#701 and #689 are held identically** — open, with a stated entry condition — so the board does
   not encode the same situation two ways.

## Second half — the operator's rig pass, and what it found

The session continued after the docs were committed: the operator ran #652's scenarios on the rig
while the coordinator diagnosed. Three defects filed, one mu-plugin extended, one of my own claims
retracted.

**#652 scenarios 3 and 4a PASSED** — the card's worst fear did not reproduce. `address_field` and
`postcode_field` set to `hide_for_pickup` hid the fields and did NOT block the order; measured
`required=false` on both. What blocked the order was something else entirely.

**#707 — DaData's federal cities break the region→settlement invariant.** Popular settlements were
seeded for DaData from LIVE `suggest()` responses rather than hand-written rows (the phone-mask
lesson). The seeding measurement itself found the defect: Moscow and St Petersburg come back with
`ancestors = []`, and their region and settlement records carry the SAME `fias_guid`. The standing
rule (#551/#552) — "a region whose `key()` is not in the settlement's `ancestors()` is refused" —
therefore cannot pass for the two most common cities in any Russian shop. That is the operator's
"works, but not as cleanly as CDEK".

**#709 — "is this a pickup method?" has FOUR independent declarations and no cross-check.** The
operator found the asymmetry: one rig method shows the pickup button but does not hide the address;
the other hides the address but has no button. Measured: `is_pickup_shipping()` (drives the field
policy), `Pickup_Field::create()` (drives the button), `set_requires_pickup_methods()` (the server
backstop) and `Selection_Scope::type_for_method()` are four separate sources. The framework's own
docblock at `class-pickup-handler.php:2740` already notes the list "is declared twice elsewhere by
the plugin" — the right conclusion, but nothing reconciles the declarations that do exist.

**#708 — and here I got it backwards, publicly.** I filed it as "a field hidden in WooCommerce
settings is resurrected onto the checkout, required". The operator corrected me: he never said he
SAW those fields, only that he got ERRORS about them. The truth is the inverse and sharper:
`inject()` skips takeover fields from rendering while `validate()` enforces `required` on every
declared descriptor, so the fields are enforced without ever being rendered — unfillable, and the
order is blocked permanently. A probe reproduced his two error strings verbatim. The card was
rewritten, its inverted body preserved as a comment, and the rig wiki line corrected.

**The rig could not express a correct pickup method, and now can.** The container-only mu-plugin was
extended to declare `woodev_test_pickup_shipping` in the two registries reachable through public API
after the fixture builds its handler (`Checkout_Fields::add()`, documented as add-or-replace;
`set_requires_pickup_methods()`). Verified by reading the stored condition spec back. The fourth
declaration is a private constructor argument with no seam — so a point chosen under that method is
still not restored across a reload, which is itself evidence for #709.
